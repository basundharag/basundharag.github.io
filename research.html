Research

Understanding the Universe as we know it

The big picture

I study the evolution of the Universe as we know it, with a focus on the later epochs when structures such as galaxies and clusters formed, and on the present era of accelerated cosmic expansion.

PhD days

During my PhD, I worked on cosmological observables and the effects that shape them. Some of the questions I addressed:

  • How do we obtain a full-sky description of cosmological observables?
  • How does weak gravitational lensing affect observables such as galaxy number counts?
  • How can we test gravity on cosmological scales using actual observables?
  • How can we model observables on smaller, non-linear scales?
  • Are cosmological observables invariant under changes of conformal frame?
  • How do local astrophysical effects, such as intrinsic alignments, affect our measurements?

What now?

My current interests span threads from my PhD work as well as new directions. Some recent topics:

  • The effects of a disformal transformation on cosmological observables
  • Higher-order gravitational tidal shear contributions (flexions) and their effect on weak lensing signals
  • Can the distance-duality relation be violated for lensing in non-metric spacetimes, and with intrinsic alignments?
  • Can a single unified linear alignment model describe both elliptical and spiral galaxies?
  • How might dynamical dark energy models within modified gravity theories help resolve the Hubble tension?
  • Are the BAO measurements from DESI and SDSS consistent with each other?

What next?

  • Can combining early-time and late-time models better alleviate the Hubble tension?
  • How viable are such models for the S8 tension?
  • How could the linear alignment model be used to test gravity?
